Features

Your Dock, one flick up

Pin the apps you want, in the order you want. Click to open, or to bring it forward if it's already running.

Dock-style magnification

Icons swell as the pointer passes over them, just like the real Dock. Turn it up, down or off.

Downloads stack

Your twelve newest downloads, newest first, with their icons. One more item opens the whole folder.

Hide the macOS Dock

Settings copies the command and opens Terminal. Paste, press Return, and the Dock stays away, even at the bottom edge. Bringing it back works the same way.

Running dots

A small dot sits under every app that's open, updated the moment an app launches or quits.

Tuned to your bar

Icon size, spacing and magnification are live sliders. The strip resizes as you drag, and never grows taller than your menu bar.

How It Works

Two minutes of setup, then it's just there.

Pick your apps

Right-click the strip and choose Settings. Add apps from your Applications folder or from what's open right now, and drag to reorder.

Good to Know

Can the strip sit on the left or in the middle?

No. macOS puts menu bar items like LilDock on the right, with Wi-Fi and the clock. Hold ⌘ and drag the strip to move it among the other icons there; it remembers where you left it.

Why does hiding the Dock go through Terminal?

LilDock is sandboxed for the App Store, so it isn't allowed to change another app's settings, the Dock included. It copies the one-line command for you instead, and the Show button undoes it.

Does it need any permissions?

No prompts at all. It doesn't use Accessibility or Screen Recording and doesn't read other apps' settings. It can see the apps you pick and your Downloads folder, read-only, and that's it.

My MacBook has a notch. Will it fit?

The icons size themselves to your menu bar's height. The space left of the notch is limited, though, and macOS hides items that don't fit, so keep the list to the apps you really reach for.

Will it slow my Mac down?

It's one small menu bar item. It never polls or runs timers; it only wakes up when an app launches or quits, or when you point at it.

LilDock is a small independent app from LilStudio. It isn't affiliated with Apple, and it doesn't replace or modify the macOS Dock.
