    Features

    Everything a focus dimmer should do, and a few things they usually don't.

    Dim or Blur

    A solid tint in any color you like, or a soft blur. Pick the strength with a slider, from barely there to 90%.

    Light & Dark Profiles

    Separate intensity and color for Light and Dark mode. When macOS switches, LilDim fades gently to match.

    One Window, or the Whole App

    Highlight just the front window, or every window of the front app. Choose per app, too.

    Made for Big Setups

    Every display handled: keep one focused window and dim the rest, or keep one bright window per display.

    Nothing Wrongly Dimmed

    Save sheets, dialogs, menus, popovers and tooltips stay bright, with perfect corners and shadows. Launchers and menu bar popovers never flash the screen.

    Focus Ring

    Add a colored border around the window you're in. Use it with the dim, or on its own at 0% intensity.

    Pause Rules

    Pause for 15 minutes, an hour, or until tomorrow. Or pause automatically whenever an app you choose is in front, like Keynote or a game.

    Hold fn to Peek

    Hold the fn key and the dim lifts until you let go. Handy for a quick look, or when dragging a file between windows.

    Fast Controls

    Scroll on the menu bar icon to adjust, ⌥-click to toggle, ⌃⌥⌘D from anywhere. Shortcuts for intensity and highlight mode too.

    Automate Everything

    Shortcuts actions, Focus Filters, AppleScript and a lildim:// URL scheme. Dim harder during Work focus, turn off for Gaming.

    Polite to Your Mac

    Keeps the menu bar bright (macOS Tahoe's see-through one included), and optionally the Dock. Respects Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency and Low Power Mode.

    Light as a Feather

    No network access, no tracking, nothing to sign up for. Moving windows costs nothing: the dim sits right behind your window instead of chasing it.

    URL scheme

    From Terminal, Raycast, Alfred or a Stream Deck button.

    open "lildim://toggle"

open "lildim://pause?minutes=30"

    Good to Know

    Does LilDim need any permissions?

    No. It works the moment you open it, using only the public window list (positions and sizes, never titles or contents). Accessibility is optional: with it, LilDim follows focus changes instantly and knows exactly which of an app's windows is focused, and fn-to-peek turns on.

    I can't find LilDim in the Accessibility list.

    macOS doesn't let sandboxed apps add themselves there. In System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, click + and choose LilDim (or drag it in from Finder), then switch it on. LilDim's Settings walks you through it and turns green the moment it's done.

    Will it dim my menus, dialogs or Save sheets?

    No. The dim sits directly behind your window in the system's window order, so everything that belongs to it (sheets, menus, popovers, tooltips) stays above it and bright. Moving or resizing the window costs no CPU at all.

    Is it heavy on battery?

    No. In our tests it sat at about 0.1% CPU while idle without Accessibility, around 25 MB of memory, and the app is about 2 MB. Blur switches to dim on its own in Low Power Mode.

    Coming from HazeOver?

    Everything you used is here: intensity, color, fade speed, Light and Dark profiles, front window or all app windows, multi-display modes, Focus Filters, Shortcuts, AppleScript and hold fn. LilDim adds blur, a focus ring, per-app pause rules and a URL scheme, for free.

    I hid the menu bar icon. How do I get back?

    Open LilDim again from Finder, Spotlight or Launchpad and Settings appears.

    Why do screenshots and recordings show the dim?

    Because it's really on screen. Pause LilDim (or hold fn) while you capture if you want the full desktop.
